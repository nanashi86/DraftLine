How DraftLine compares

DraftLine brings professional screenplay formatting, story development and production planning into one free, open-source writing app. Write on a computer or a phone, work offline, and keep your scripts without an account, a subscription or AI-generated writing.

Nothing is held back for paying users, because there are none: the Scene Board with live act pacing, the Story Bible, Final Draft compatibility and production breakdowns are all in the free app.

DraftLine is designed first for independent writers. If your work needs simultaneous co-writing, locked shooting scripts with colored revision pages, or full scheduling and budgeting, the established tools below offer what DraftLine doesn't currently provide. Here's how the options compare.

Where DraftLine stands out

  • Free means everything. No subscription, no account, no paid tier, and no page or project limits set by DraftLine. Professional pagination, Final Draft files, the Scene Board, Story Bible, Format Doctor, Dialogue Tuner, snapshots and production planning are in the one free app.
  • Your story, your voice. DraftLine doesn't write for you: it has no generative AI, and nothing in it drafts, rewrites or suggests your story or dialogue. Its checks and counts are rule-based readings of your own pages: Format Doctor flags format problems and offers fixes, Dialogue Tuner shows each character's lines with monologue and cadence statistics, and the Scene Board can suggest each scene's value shift. You decide what to apply.
  • Story development beside the script. The Scene Board's cards sit in 3- or 4-act columns and are linked to their scenes: drag a card and its whole scene moves in the screenplay. Live act pacing shows each act's pages and share of the script against the usual share (25 / 50 / 25 in three acts, a quarter each in four), and updates as you move scenes or rewrite. The Story Bible holds the story's DNA (genre, theme, central dramatic question, plot devices and eight story questions), notes such as the logline, and a screenwriting glossary; Characters holds bios and every line each character speaks.
  • Production preparation from the same pages. Plan reads the script into scene breakdowns (INT/EXT, day or night, length in eighths, speaking cast with numbers, plus your lists of extras, props, wardrobe and vehicles), a color-coded stripboard, and reports on scenes, characters, locations and the whole script, each exportable as CSV.
  • Professional pages, even on a phone. Strict 54-line pagination with (MORE) and (CONT'D), and a PDF built to match the screen. On a phone you write in a readable 16px view with an element bar above the keyboard, and the page breaks stay exactly as they are on a computer: a script continued on a phone is the same script, page for page. More about writing on a phone.
  • Your scripts stay in your hands. Work offline, without an account. Scripts are kept in your browser, or as files in a folder you link (Chrome or Edge on a computer), and open and save as Final Draft (.fdx), Fountain and a complete .draftline backup.

Where others are the better choice

  • Locked shooting scripts. Once a script is locked for production, revisions go out as colored pages with revision marks, locked pages and omitted scenes. Final Draft, Fade In and WriterDuet's Pro plan are built for that. DraftLine keeps drafts as snapshots you can compare, but doesn't produce revision pages.
  • Writing together, live. Two writers in one script at the same moment is what WriterDuet, Celtx and Final Draft do, and Fade In through its optional online service. DraftLine has no shared cloud document: a linked folder carries a script between your own computers, and the latest save wins.
  • Scheduling and budgeting. DraftLine prepares breakdowns, strips and reports, but doesn't schedule shoot days, build budgets or make call sheets. Celtx's paid plans do.
  • A native app on Mac and iPhone. If you only write on Apple devices and want a native, open-source app, Beat is excellent.
