How DraftLine compares

DraftLine is built for one writer and one script: free, open source, in the browser on a computer or a phone, offline, with no account. If that's you, it's a good fit. If you need a team workflow or production drafts, one of the tools below will serve you better, and this page says which.

Where others are the better choice

  • Production drafts. Once a script is locked for shooting, productions issue colored revision pages (blue, pink, yellow…) with revision marks. DraftLine doesn't do this; Final Draft, Fade In and WriterDuet do.
  • Writing together live. DraftLine has no shared cloud document. Two writers in one script at the same time is what WriterDuet, Final Draft and Celtx are built for. A linked folder lets you move between your own computers, but the most recent save wins; edits aren't merged.
  • Mac and iPhone only, also open source. If you only write on a Mac or an iPhone, Beat is excellent.
  • A full production suite. Celtx's paid plans add scheduling, budgeting and call sheets. DraftLine's Plan view covers breakdowns, a stripboard and reports worked out from the script, not scheduling or budgets.

Where DraftLine fits

  • Free, with nothing held back. Every tool is in the free app: pagination, Final Draft and Fountain files, PDF, Scene Board, Format Doctor, Dialogue Tuner, Story Bible, snapshots and Plan.
  • Exact pages everywhere. Strict 54-line Hollywood pagination with (MORE) and (CONT'D). The same page breaks on a computer, on a phone, in the PDF and in exports.
  • Writing on a phone. A readable 16px writing view with an element bar above the keyboard, in the phone's browser, with no app store. More about writing on a phone.
  • Your files, on your device. No account, works offline, and scripts stay in your browser or in a folder you link (Chrome or Edge on a computer), in open formats.
